Abstract

From final outcomes to internal representations.

Large Language Models (LLMs) demonstrate remarkable reasoning capabilities, yet the internal mechanisms driving these multi-step processes remain opaque. Existing mechanistic interpretability approaches often rely on superficial text-pattern co-occurrences or capture only short-term effects, struggling to trace the long-horizon, outcome-driven influence of internal components such as neurons or sparse features in multi-step reasoning. We introduce Integrated Policy Gradient (IPG), a training-free framework that brings policy-based attribution into mechanistic interpretability for LLM reasoning. IPG uses outcome-derived advantages to attribute behavioral signals across inference trajectories and integrates gradients along baseline-to-activation paths. Aggregated attributions identify outcome-relevant components for interpretation and intervention. Empirical evaluations on representative open-source LLMs demonstrate localization of sparse subsets of components, complementary spatial, semantic, and temporal insights, and improvements in reasoning performance through inference-time modulation.

Integrated attribution

Baseline-to-activation paths

Accumulate gradients as component activations move from a baseline to their original values, then combine contributions across generation steps.

Inference-time control

Selected-component scaling

Aggregate IPG scores across questions and select top-ranked components. Scale their activations by γ to suppress or enhance their influence.

Technical interpretation

IPG is an outcome-oriented, sequential attribution framework motivated by a linear approximation of mediation effects. Its gradient-based scores are evaluated through component interventions and alignment with a group-based zero-ablation mediation proxy; they are not exact counterfactual mediation estimates.
